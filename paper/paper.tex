\noindent\textbf{Abstract:} We prove that for every sufficiently large $n$, every set
$A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ with more than $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+\lfloor n/3\rfloor-\lfloor n/6\rfloor$
elements has a coprime graph containing cycles of all odd lengths from $3$ to $n/3+1$. This answers
a question of Erd\H{o}s and S\'ark\"ozy (Erd\H{o}s Problem 883). The proof is formally verified in
Lean~4.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

For a finite set $A$ of positive integers, the \emph{coprime graph} $G(A)$ has vertex set $A$, two
distinct elements being adjacent when they are coprime. A cycle of length $L\ge 3$ in $G(A)$ is a
sequence of $L$ distinct elements $v_1,\dots,v_L$ of $A$ such that $v_i$ is coprime to $v_{i+1}$ for
$i<L$ and $v_L$ is coprime to $v_1$. Write $[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}$ and
\[
  T(n)=\Big\lfloor\frac n2\Big\rfloor+\Big\lfloor\frac n3\Big\rfloor-\Big\lfloor\frac n6\Big\rfloor
\]
for the number of elements of $[n]$ divisible by $2$ or by $3$. The set $M(n)$ of these elements
spans no triangle, since among any three of them two are even or two are multiples of
$3$.\footnote{The extremal role of the multiples of the first primes goes back to Erd\H{o}s
\cite{Erdos62}. Let $f(n,k)$ be the number of integers in $[n]$ divisible by at least one of the first
$k$ primes, so that $f(n,2)=T(n)$. Erd\H{o}s asked whether every $A\subseteq[n]$ with $|A|>f(n,k)$
contains $k+1$ pairwise coprime integers, that is, a complete subgraph on $k+1$ vertices in $G(A)$.
For $k=2$ the answer is yes (for $n\ge13$ this is Theorem~\ref{thm:triangle} below), while Ahlswede
and Khachatrian \cite{AK94} showed that the answer is negative for some larger $k$. Erd\H{o}s and
S\'ark\"ozy \cite{ES97} replaced complete subgraphs by odd cycles.}

Erd\H{o}s and S\'ark\"ozy \cite{ES97} proved that one additional element forces much more than a
triangle: there is a constant $c>0$ such that, for every large $n$ and every $A\subseteq[n]$ with
$|A|>T(n)$, the graph $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $2l+1$ for every positive integer $l\le cn$.
They asked whether $c=1/6$ is admissible. Since $l\le n/6$ means $2l+1\le n/3+1$, this is
Question~1 of Erd\H{o}s Problem~883 \cite{Bloom}.\footnote{Erd\H{o}s and S\'ark\"ozy state their
theorem, and the question, for $n\ge n_0$. The problem list \cite{Bloom} records the question without
the words ``for large $n$''. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} answers the question in the form posed in
\cite{ES97}, for all $n$ from some point on; it says nothing about small $n$.} We answer the
question.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is an integer $n_0$ such that for every $n\ge n_0$ and every $A\subseteq[n]$ with $|A|>T(n)$,
the coprime graph $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $L$ for every odd $L$ with $3\le L\le n/3+1$.
\end{theorem}

The size condition is sharp, and so is the range of lengths when $6\mid n$. The set $M(n)$ shows
that $|A|>T(n)$ cannot be weakened. Two even numbers are never adjacent, so the even vertices of a cycle of odd
length $L$ are pairwise non-consecutive and there are at most $(L-1)/2$ of them; such a cycle
contains at least $(L+1)/2$ odd numbers. The set $M(n)\cup\{1\}$ has $T(n)+1$ elements but only
$o(n)+1$ odd ones, where
\[
  o(n)=\Big\lfloor\frac n3\Big\rfloor-\Big\lfloor\frac n6\Big\rfloor
\]
is the number of odd multiples of $3$ in $[n]$, so its odd cycles have length at most $2o(n)+1$, which
equals $n/3+1$ when $6\mid n$.

\paragraph{The approach.} Let $d$ be the number of integers $w\le n$ with $w\equiv 2,4\pmod 6$ that
are missing from $A$. When $70d\le n$, the even numbers prime to $3$ that lie in $A$ form a large
reservoir of connecting vertices. Every odd length from $5$ to $2o(n)+1$ is then obtained by joining
short paths through odd vertices with the help of Jackson's theorem on cycles in bipartite graphs
(Theorem~\ref{thm:few}). When $70d>n$ this reservoir is too small. Depending on how many multiples of
$2$ or $3$ are missing from $A$, we either use all multiples of $2$ or $3$ that lie in $A$ as
connectors between units of $A$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:unit}), or keep the even numbers prime to $3$
and add one long path that alternates between units of $A$ and multiples of $6$ in $A$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gp}). Both constructions need many elements $z$ of $A$ with a large value of
\[
  \rho'(z)=\prod_{p\mid z,\ p\ge5}\Big(1-\frac1p\Big),
\]
and the exceptions in each residue class modulo $6$ are bounded by sixteen explicit density
certificates (Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}). A finite cover of the parameter space by boxes
(Section~\ref{sec:boxes}) shows that one of the two constructions applies in every case.
Figure~\ref{fig:flow} shows the structure of the proof.

\paragraph{Formal verification.} Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is proved in Lean~4 with the Mathlib library
\cite{Lean4,Mathlib}. The formal statement is quoted in Section~\ref{sec:lean}. Its proof uses only
the three standard axioms of Lean and Mathlib, and every numerical inequality, the sixteen
certificates included, is checked by the Lean kernel.\footnote{Lean is an interactive theorem prover
based on dependent type theory. A proof is accepted only after the kernel, a small program that
implements the typing rules, has checked every step. The command \texttt{\#print axioms} lists the
axioms a theorem depends on; for Theorem~\ref{thm:main} the list consists of propositional
extensionality, the axiom of choice and the soundness of quotients, which every development based on
Mathlib uses. The numerical certificates are evaluated by the kernel's own reduction
(\texttt{decide +kernel}) and do not rely on compiled code.} The text follows the formal proof step
by step.

\section{Notation and counting in residue classes}\label{sec:prelim}

Throughout, $p$ denotes a prime. For an integer $m\ge1$ let $\omega'(m)$ be the number of primes
$p\ge5$ dividing $m$, and let $\rho'(m)=\prod_{p\mid m,\,p\ge5}(1-1/p)$, with $\rho'(m)=1$ when
$\omega'(m)=0$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Residue classes modulo $6$}{Residue classes modulo 6}}

We split $[n]$ into the classes
\[
  U(n)=\{z:z\equiv\pm1\},\quad O_3(n)=\{z:z\equiv 3\},\quad \Es(n)=\{z:z\equiv2,4\},\quad
  M_6(n)=\{z:z\equiv0\},
\]
congruences being modulo $6$. Thus $M(n)=O_3(n)\cup\Es(n)\cup M_6(n)=[n]\setminus U(n)$ and
$|M(n)|=T(n)$, while $|\Es(n)|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor-\lfloor n/6\rfloor$ and
$|M_6(n)|=\lfloor n/6\rfloor$. Elements of $U(n)$ are called \emph{units}. For $A\subseteq[n]$ we
write
\[
  d=|\Es(n)\setminus A|,\qquad \mu_6=|M_6(n)\setminus A|,\qquad \mu_T=|M(n)\setminus A|,\qquad
  u=|A\cap U(n)|.
\]
Whether two integers can be coprime is decided by their residues modulo $6$: they cannot be coprime
when both are even or both are multiples of $3$. Formally, let $c\colon[n]\to\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$
be reduction modulo $6$, let $\gamma(x,y)=1$ if $\gcd(x,y,6)=1$ and $\gamma(x,y)=0$ otherwise, and
define $\bar\gamma$ on $(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})^2$ in the same way. Since $\gcd(x,y,6)$ depends only on
$x$ and $y$ modulo $6$, the triangle
\begin{equation}\label{cd:classes}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
  {[n]\times[n]} \arrow[r, "c\times c"] \arrow[dr, "\gamma"'] & (\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})^2
  \arrow[d, "\bar\gamma"] \\
  & \{0,1\}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
commutes, and $\gcd(x,y)=1$ implies $\gamma(x,y)=1$. Figure~\ref{fig:classes} shows the pairs of
classes on which $\bar\gamma=1$. The classes inside $M(n)$ are joined only by the edge between $O_3$ and
$\Es$, which is another way to see that $G(M(n))$ is bipartite and has no triangle.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:odd}
Let $A\subseteq[n]$ with $|A|>T(n)$, and let $D$ be a set of even numbers in $[n]$ disjoint from $A$.
Then $A$ has at least $o(n)+1+|D|$ odd elements.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The even elements of $A$ lie among the $\lfloor n/2\rfloor-|D|$ even numbers of $[n]$ outside $D$.
Hence $A$ has at least $T(n)+1-\lfloor n/2\rfloor+|D|=o(n)+1+|D|$ odd elements.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting coprime elements of a class}

A set $R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ is \emph{symmetric} if $R=-R$. For a finite set $P$ of
positive integers and an integer $x\ge0$ put
\[
  C_R(x;P)=\#\{w\in[x]:\ w\bmod 6\in R,\ p\nmid w\text{ for all }p\in P\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:count}
Let $R$ be symmetric and let $c_R\ge0$ satisfy $|C_R(x;\emptyset)-x|R|/6|\le c_R$ for all integers
$x\ge0$. Let $P$ be a finite set of pairwise coprime integers, each $\equiv\pm1\pmod 6$. Then for every
$n\ge0$,
\[
  \Big|C_R(n;P)-\frac n6\,|R|\prod_{p\in P}\Big(1-\frac1p\Big)\Big|
  \le\Big(c_R+\frac{|R|}6\Big)2^{|P|}-\frac{|R|}6 .
\]
One may take $c_R=5|R|/6$ for every $R$, and $c_R=2/3$ for $R=\{2,4\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The class of $r$ meets $[x]$ in $\lfloor(x+6-r')/6\rfloor$ elements, where $r'\in\{1,\dots,6\}$
represents $r$; this differs from $x/6$ by at most $5/6$, which gives $c_R=5|R|/6$. For
$R=\{2,4\}$ we have $C_R(x;\emptyset)=\lfloor x/2\rfloor-\lfloor x/6\rfloor$, which differs from
$x/3$ by at most $2/3$.

We argue by induction on $|P|$; the case $P=\emptyset$ is the hypothesis. Let $q\notin P$ be coprime
to every element of $P$, with $q\equiv\pm1\pmod 6$. Multiplication by $q$ maps $[\lfloor
n/q\rfloor]$ bijectively onto the multiples of $q$ in $[n]$, and since $q\equiv\pm1$ and $R=-R$, the
square
\begin{equation}\label{cd:mult}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
  {[\lfloor n/q\rfloor]} \arrow[r, "w\mapsto qw"] \arrow[d, "\bmod 6"'] & {[n]} \arrow[d, "\bmod 6"] \\
  R \arrow[r, "r\mapsto qr"'] & R
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
is defined on the elements whose residues lie in $R$ and commutes. For $p\in P$ we have $p\mid qw$ if
and only if $p\mid w$, because $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Hence the elements counted by $C_R(n;P)$ that are
multiples of $q$ are exactly the numbers $qw$ with $w$ counted by $C_R(\lfloor n/q\rfloor;P)$, and
\[
  C_R(n;P\cup\{q\})=C_R(n;P)-C_R(\lfloor n/q\rfloor;P).
\]
Write $\Pi=\prod_{p\in P}(1-1/p)\in(0,1]$ and $E_P(x)=|C_R(x;P)-x|R|\Pi/6|$. The main term for
$P\cup\{q\}$ equals $n|R|\Pi/6-(n/q)|R|\Pi/6$, and $|(n/q)-\lfloor n/q\rfloor|<1$. Therefore
\[
  E_{P\cup\{q\}}(n)\le E_P(n)+E_P(\lfloor n/q\rfloor)+\frac{|R|}6 ,
\]
and the induction hypothesis, applied at $n$ and at $\lfloor n/q\rfloor$, gives
\[
  E_{P\cup\{q\}}(n)\le2\Big(\Big(c_R+\frac{|R|}6\Big)2^{|P|}-\frac{|R|}6\Big)+\frac{|R|}6
  =\Big(c_R+\frac{|R|}6\Big)2^{|P|+1}-\frac{|R|}6. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

Write $\mathcal{P}(m)$ for the set of primes $p\ge5$ dividing $m$, and
$N^*(n,m)=\{w\in\Es(n):\gcd(w,m)=1\}$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:count}
Let $m\ge1$ and let $R$ be symmetric. Suppose that every element of $R$ is odd if $2\mid m$, and
prime to $3$ if $3\mid m$. Then
\[
  \Big|\#\{w\in[n]:w\bmod6\in R,\ \gcd(w,m)=1\}-\frac n6|R|\rho'(m)\Big|\le|R|\Big(2^{\omega'(m)}-
  \frac16\Big).
\]
For odd $m$, moreover, $\big||N^*(n,m)|-\tfrac n3\rho'(m)\big|\le 2^{\omega'(m)}-\tfrac13$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Under the hypothesis, an integer $w$ with $w\bmod 6\in R$ is coprime to $m$ exactly when no prime of
$\mathcal{P}(m)$ divides it, so the count equals $C_R(n;\mathcal{P}(m))$. Every prime $p\ge5$ is
$\equiv\pm1\pmod6$, and $\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}(m)}(1-1/p)=\rho'(m)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:count} with
$c_R=5|R|/6$, and with $c_R=2/3$ for $R=\{2,4\}$, gives both bounds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The density $\rho'$}{The density rho'}}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rho}
Let $a,b,m,z\ge1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
\item $\rho'(m)\ge1/(\omega'(m)+1)$ and $(\omega'(m)+1)!\le m$.
\item If $z\mid m$, then $\rho'(m)\le\rho'(z)$.
\item $\rho'(\lcm(a,b))\ge\rho'(a)\rho'(b)$.
\item $N^*(n,\lcm(a,b))=N^*(n,a)\cap N^*(n,b)$, that is, the square of inclusions
\begin{equation}\label{cd:pullback}
\begin{tikzcd}
  N^*(n,\lcm(a,b)) \arrow[r, hook] \arrow[d, hook] & N^*(n,a) \arrow[d, hook] \\
  N^*(n,b) \arrow[r, hook] & \Es(n)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
is a pullback.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Let $p_1<\dots<p_K$ be the primes of $\mathcal{P}(m)$, $K=\omega'(m)$. Then $p_i\ge i+1$, so
$\rho'(m)\ge\prod_{i\le K}(1-1/(i+1))=1/(K+1)$ and $m\ge\prod_i p_i\ge(K+1)!$. (b) holds because
$\mathcal{P}(z)\subseteq\mathcal{P}(m)$ and every factor is at most $1$. (c) Since
$\mathcal{P}(\lcm(a,b))=\mathcal{P}(a)\cup\mathcal{P}(b)$, the product over the union equals the
product over $\mathcal{P}(a)$ times the product over $\mathcal{P}(b)$ divided by the product over
$\mathcal{P}(a)\cap\mathcal{P}(b)$, which is at most $1$. (d) An integer is coprime to $\lcm(a,b)$ if
and only if it is coprime to $a$ and to $b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:large}
Let $N_0=1000\cdot23\cdot2^{22}$ and $N_1=10^6\cdot30\cdot2^{29}$, and let $1\le m\le
n^2$. If
$n\ge N_0$, then $1000(\omega'(m)+1)2^{\omega'(m)}\le n$. If $n\ge N_1$, then
$10^6(\omega'(m)+1)2^{\omega'(m)}\le n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $K=\omega'(m)$ and $h(K)=1000(K+1)2^K$. If $K\le 21$, then $h(K)\le N_0$. For $K\ge22$ we show
$h(K)^2\le(K+1)!$; together with Lemma~\ref{lem:rho}(a) this gives $h(K)^2\le m\le n^2$. For $K=22$
the inequality reads $(9.65\ldots\cdot10^{10})^2\le23!=2.58\ldots\cdot10^{22}$. Passing from $K$ to
$K+1$ multiplies the left side by $4(K+2)^2/(K+1)^2$ and the right side by $K+2$, and
$4(K+2)\le(K+1)^2$ for $K\ge4$. The second statement is proved in the same way with $10^6$ in place
of $1000$, the cut at $K=29$, and $(10^6\cdot30\cdot2^{29})^2=2.594\ldots\cdot10^{32}\le30!
=2.652\ldots\cdot10^{32}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:large}
Let $1\le m\le n^2$. If $n\ge N_0$, then $2^{\omega'(m)}\le n\rho'(m)/1000$. If $n\ge N_1$, then
$2^{\omega'(m)}\le n/10^6$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemma~\ref{lem:large} with $(\omega'(m)+1)\rho'(m)\ge1$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:rho}(a).
\end{proof}

No attempt has been made to optimise $N_0$ and $N_1$.\footnote{Numerically
$N_0=96\,468\,992\,000\approx9.6\cdot10^{10}$ and $N_1\approx1.6\cdot10^{16}$. Both come from the crude
bound $(\omega'(m)+1)!\le m$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:rho}(a), which keeps the proof free of analytic
estimates.}

\subsection{Two combinatorial lemmas}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pair}
Let $n\ge13$ and let $O$ be a set of at least $o(n)+1$ odd numbers in $[n]$. Then $O$ contains two
distinct coprime elements.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $i\ge0$ call $\{6i+1,6i+3,6i+5\}$ the $i$-th block. Two distinct odd numbers in one block differ
by $2$ or $4$, so their greatest common divisor divides $4$ and is $1$. Suppose that $O$ contains no
coprime pair; then each block contains at most one element of $O$. The blocks meeting $[n]$ are those
with $i\le K=\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$, and $o(n)=\lfloor(n+3)/6\rfloor\ge K$. Hence $|O|\ge o(n)+1\ge
K+1$ forces $o(n)=K$ and one element of $O$ in every block. The equality
$\lfloor(n+3)/6\rfloor=\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$ means $n\equiv1,2\pmod 6$, so the last block meets
$[n]$ only in $6K+1$, which therefore lies in $O$. The element $x_0$ of $O$ in block $0$ is $1$, $3$
or $5$, and the element $x_1$ in block $1$ is $7$, $9$ or $11$ (here $K\ge2$ as $n\ge13$). Since $1$
is coprime to everything and $5$ is coprime to $7$, $9$ and $11$, we get $x_0=3$. But $3$ is coprime
to $6K+1\ne3$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:greedy}
Let $I$ be a finite set with a map $\nu$ into a linearly ordered set, and for each $i\in I$ let
$C_i$ be a finite set with $|C_i|\ge\#\{j\in I:\nu(j)\le\nu(i)\}$. Then there are pairwise
distinct elements $w_i\in C_i$ $(i\in I)$.\footnote{The conclusion says that the family
$(C_i)_{i\in I}$ has a system of distinct representatives. Hall's theorem \cite{Hall35} characterises
such families completely; the hypothesis here implies Hall's condition and allows the
greedy proof below, which is also the one used in Lean.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose the $w_i$ in order of increasing $\nu(i)$, breaking ties arbitrarily. When $i$ is reached, the values already chosen
belong to indices $j\ne i$ with $\nu(j)\le\nu(i)$, and there are fewer than $|C_i|$ of them.
\end{proof}

\section{Jackson's theorem and the assembly lemma}\label{sec:jackson}

\begin{theorem}[Jackson \cite{Jackson81}]\label{thm:jackson}
Let $H$ be a bipartite graph with parts $X$ and $Y$, and let $k$ be an integer with $2\le|X|\le k$ and
$|Y|\le2k-2$. If every vertex of $X$ has at least $k$ neighbours, then $H$ has a cycle through all
vertices of $X$.
\end{theorem}

We include a short proof.\footnote{Jackson's theorem belongs to the family of degree conditions for
long cycles that started with Dirac's theorem \cite{Dirac52}: a graph on $N\ge3$ vertices with
minimum degree at least $N/2$ has a Hamiltonian cycle. Erd\H{o}s and S\'ark\"ozy used
it in \cite{ES97}, and it is the engine of all constructions in this paper. The argument given here,
a maximal cycle together with one counting inequality, is the one formalized in Lean.}

\begin{proof}
Two vertices of $X$ have at least $2k-|Y|\ge2$ common neighbours, which gives a cycle of length $4$
through two vertices of $X$. It suffices to show that a cycle
$C=x_0y_0x_1y_1\cdots x_{t-1}y_{t-1}x_0$ with $x_i\in X$, $y_i\in Y$ and $t<|X|$ can be replaced by
such a cycle through $t+1$ vertices of $X$. Indices are taken modulo $t$, and $t\le k-1$.

Fix $x\in X$ not on $C$. Let $Y'=Y\setminus\{y_0,\dots,y_{t-1}\}$, so $|Y'|=|Y|-t$, and for $v\in X$
let $N'(v)$ be the set of neighbours of $v$ in $Y'$. Let $I=\{i:x\sim y_i\}$ and $s=|I|$. Suppose that
$C$ cannot be enlarged.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), nosep]
\item For $i\in I$ the sets $N'(x)$ and $N'(x_{i+1})$ are disjoint: a common element $y$ would give the
cycle in which the edge $y_ix_{i+1}$ is replaced by the path $y_i\,x\,y\,x_{i+1}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:jackson}(a)).
\item For distinct $i,j\in I$ the sets $N'(x_{i+1})$ and $N'(x_{j+1})$ are disjoint: a common element
$y$ would give the cycle
\[
  x\ y_i\ x_i\ y_{i-1}\cdots y_{j+1}\ x_{j+1}\ y\ x_{i+1}\ y_{i+1}\cdots x_j\ y_j\ x,
\]
which runs backwards along $C$ from $x_i$ to $x_{j+1}$ and forwards from $x_{i+1}$ to $x_j$
(Figure~\ref{fig:jackson}(b)).
\end{enumerate}
The vertices $x_{i+1}$, $i\in I$, are distinct. The vertex $x$ has exactly $s$ neighbours on $C$, so
$|N'(x)|\ge k-s$, and $|N'(x_{i+1})|\ge k-t\ge1$. If $s\ge1$, the disjoint sets in (i) and (ii) give
\[
  (k-s)+s(k-t)\le|Y'|\le2k-2-t .
\]
As $s(k-t)\ge(k-t)+(s-1)$, the left side is at least $2k-t-1$, a contradiction. If $s=0$, then $x$ has
no neighbour on $C$. Choose $y'$ adjacent to $x$ and $x_0$, and $y''\ne y'$ adjacent to $x$ and
$x_1$; both exist because any two vertices of $X$ have at least two common neighbours, and both lie
off $C$. Replacing $x_0y_0x_1$ by $x_0y'xy''x_1$ enlarges $C$ (Figure~\ref{fig:jackson}(c)).
\end{proof}

open ones in $Y$; thick edges are new and dashed edges are removed.}
\label{fig:jackson}
\end{figure}

In our applications the vertices of $X$ are paths in the coprime graph, and the vertices of $Y$ are
single integers that join them.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:assembly}
Let $A$ be a finite set of positive integers and $k$ an integer. Let $X$ be a finite index set and,
for $c\in X$, let $P_c=(v^c_1,\dots,v^c_{m_c})$ with $m_c\ge1$ be a sequence of distinct elements of
$A$ in which consecutive terms are coprime; let the $P_c$ be pairwise disjoint. Let $B\subseteq A$ be
disjoint from all $P_c$, and call $\beta\in B$ \emph{attached} to $P_c$ if $\beta$ is coprime to
$v^c_1$ and to $v^c_{m_c}$. If $2\le|X|\le k$, $|B|\le2k-2$ and every $P_c$ has at least $k$ attached
elements of $B$, then $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $\sum_{c\in X}m_c+|X|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:jackson} to the bipartite graph $H$ on $X\cup B$ whose edges are the
attachments. It yields a cycle $c_0\beta_0c_1\beta_1\cdots c_{t-1}\beta_{t-1}c_0$ with
$\{c_0,\dots,c_{t-1}\}=X$. Replacing each $c_i$ by $P_{c_i}$ gives the closed sequence
$P_{c_0}\beta_0P_{c_1}\beta_1\cdots P_{c_{t-1}}\beta_{t-1}$ of distinct elements of $A$; consecutive
terms are coprime inside each $P_c$, and $\beta_i$ is coprime to the last term of $P_{c_i}$ and to
the first term of $P_{c_{i+1}}$. Its length is $\sum_c m_c+t\ge4$. Writing $\Phi$ for this expansion
and $\ell$ for the length of a cycle, the triangle
\begin{equation}\label{cd:assembly}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
  \{\text{cycles of }H\text{ through all of }X\} \arrow[r, "\Phi"] \arrow[dr, "\textstyle\sum_c m_c+|X|"']
  & \{\text{cycles of }G(A)\} \arrow[d, "\ell"] \\
  & \mathbb{N}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
commutes, where the diagonal map is constant.
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{How many integers have small $\rho'$}{How many integers have small rho'}}\label{sec:density}

For a finite set $\mathcal{T}$ of primes let $\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T},\,p\mid
z}(1-1/p)$, and for $m\ge0$ let $g_m(p)=(p/(p-1))^m-1\ge0$.

\begin{lemma}[moment bound]\label{lem:moment}
For every finite set $\mathcal{T}$ of primes, all integers $n,m\ge0$ and every $x>0$,
\[
  \#\{z\in[n]:\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)\le x\}\le n\,x^m\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T}}\Big(1+\frac{g_m(p)}p\Big).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)\le x$, then $1\le(x/\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z))^m=x^m\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T},\,
p\mid z}(1+g_m(p))$. Expanding the product and summing over $z\le n$,
\[
  \sum_{z\le n}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T},\,p\mid z}(1+g_m(p))=\sum_{D\subseteq\mathcal{T}}
  \Big\lfloor\frac n{\prod_{p\in D}p}\Big\rfloor\prod_{p\in D}g_m(p)\le
  n\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T}}\Big(1+\frac{g_m(p)}p\Big). \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

This is Rankin's method.\footnote{R.~A.~Rankin \cite{Rankin38} introduced the device of bounding the
number of integers with a multiplicative property by a weighted sum with an adjustable exponent, in
his work on large gaps between consecutive primes. Here the exponent $m$ is chosen from a finite
menu, separately for each signature in Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}.} The Euler products are controlled as
follows. For $s\ge5$ let
\[
  F_s(m)=\prod_{s\le p<200}\Big(1+\frac{g_m(p)}p\Big)\in\mathbb{Q}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tail}
If $2m+2\le200$ and $\mathcal{T}$ is a finite set of primes $p\ge s$, then
$\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T}}(1+g_m(p)/p)\le F_s(m)/(1-2m/199)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $p\ge2m+2$ put $v=1/(p-1)$. Bernoulli's inequality gives $1-mv\le(1-v)^m$, and
$(1+v)^m(1-v)^m\le1$, so $(1+v)^m\le1/(1-mv)$ and $g_m(p)\le mv/(1-mv)=m/(p-1-m)\le2m/p$. For any
finite set of integers $p\ge200$ we have $\sum1/p^2\le\sum(1/(p-1)-1/p)\le1/199$, and for $a_p\ge0$
the inequality $\prod(1+a_p)\cdot(1-\sum a_p)\le1$ holds by induction. With $a_p=2m/p^2$ the primes
$p\ge200$ of $\mathcal{T}$ contribute at most $1/(1-2m/199)$, and the primes below $200$ at most
$F_s(m)$, all factors being at least $1$.
\end{proof}

Call an odd number $z$ \emph{hard} if $\rho'(z)<14/25$ and \emph{easy} if $\rho'(z)\ge3/4$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hard}
For every $n\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
\item $\#\{z\in[n]:\rho'(z)\le x\}\le xn/560$ for $0<x\le14/25$; in particular at most $n/1000$
integers in $[n]$ are hard;
\item at most $11(\lfloor n/70\rfloor+1)+n/1000$ odd integers in $[n]$ are not easy.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(a) For $z\le n$ we have $\rho'(z)=\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)$ with $\mathcal{T}$ the primes in $[5,n]$.
The Lean kernel verifies the rational inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F5}
  (14/25)^{37}F_5(37)\le\tfrac1{1000}\big(1-74/199\big).
\end{equation}
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:moment} and \ref{lem:tail} with $m=37$, and $x^{37}\le x(14/25)^{36}$, the count
is at most
\[
  nx\cdot\frac{25}{14}\cdot\frac{(14/25)^{37}F_5(37)}{1-74/199}\le\frac{25}{14}\cdot\frac{nx}{1000}
  =\frac{nx}{560}.
\]

(b) Among any $70$ consecutive integers exactly $11$ are odd and divisible by $5$ or $7$, so $[n]$
contains at most $11(\lfloor n/70\rfloor+1)$ of them. For the other odd $z\le n$,
$\rho'(z)=\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)$ with $\mathcal{T}$ the primes in $[11,n]$, and the kernel verifies
$(3/4)^{54}F_{11}(54)\le\frac1{1000}(1-108/199)$; Lemmas~\ref{lem:moment} and \ref{lem:tail} with
$m=54$ bound their number by $n/1000$.
\end{proof}

The second estimate concerns residue classes and finer thresholds. Let
$P_{19}=\{5,7,11,13,17,19\}$. For $S\subseteq P_{19}$ put
\[
  \rho'(S)=\prod_{p\in S}\Big(1-\frac1p\Big),\qquad
  \operatorname{dens}(S)=\frac{\prod_{p\in P_{19}\setminus S}(1-1/p)}{\prod_{p\in S}p},
\]
the density of the integers whose set of prime divisors in $P_{19}$ is exactly $S$. Let
\[
  \mathcal{M}=\{0,3,6,10,15,20,25,30,40,50,60,70,85,99\},
\]
and let $\hat F_m$ $(m\in\mathcal{M})$ be the rationals of Table~\ref{tab:fhat}, which satisfy
$F_{23}(m)\le\hat F_m(1-2m/199)$. For
$\theta'>0$ define
\[
  \cert(\theta')=\sum_{S\subseteq P_{19}}\operatorname{dens}(S)\min_{m\in\mathcal{M}}
  \Big(\frac{\theta'}{\rho'(S)}\Big)^m\hat F_m .
\]
For a symmetric $R$ and $\theta>0$ put $\mathrm{Bad}_R(n,\theta)=\#\{z\in[n]:z\bmod6\in R,\ \rho'(z)
<\theta\}$.

\begin{theorem}[density certificates]\label{thm:cert}
Let $\theta,\theta',\beta$ be rationals with $0<\theta\le\theta'(1-10^{-4})$ and
$\cert(\theta')+7\cdot10^{-6}\le\beta$. Then there is $N$ such that for every $n\ge N$ and every
nonempty symmetric $R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$,
\[
  \mathrm{Bad}_R(n,\theta)\le\frac n6|R|\beta .
\]
The hypotheses hold for the sixteen triples of Table~\ref{tab:cert}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $n$ and $R$, and let $Y=10^{10}$ and $\eta=10^{-4}$. Figure~\ref{fig:ranges}
summarises the argument.\footnote{The small primes change $\rho'$ the most, so the $64$ signatures over $P_{19}$ are counted
exactly and only the primes from $23$ on are treated by moments. The number $Y$ enters only through
the error term $n/(\eta Y)$, while $\eta$ also enters the condition $\theta\le\theta'(1-\eta)$.} For $z\le n$ let $S_z$ be the set of primes
of $P_{19}$ dividing $z$, let $\mathcal{T}$ be the primes in $[23,Y]$ and $\mathcal{B}$ the primes in
$(Y,n]$. Splitting the primes of $\mathcal{P}(z)$ into the three ranges, that is,
$\sigma(z)=(S_z,\,\mathcal{P}(z)\cap\mathcal{T},\,\mathcal{P}(z)\cap\mathcal{B})$, gives the
commutative diagram
\begin{equation}\label{cd:split}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
  {[n]} \arrow[r, "\sigma"] \arrow[d, "\mathcal{P}"'] &
  2^{P_{19}}\times2^{\mathcal{T}}\times2^{\mathcal{B}} \arrow[d, "\psi"] \\
  2^{\{p\ge5\}} \arrow[r, "\psi_1"'] & (0,1]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
in which $\psi_1(Q)=\prod_{p\in Q}(1-1/p)$ and $\psi$ sends a triple of disjoint sets to $\psi_1$ of
their union;
hence $\rho'(z)=\rho'(S_z)\,\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)\,\varphi_\mathcal{B}(z)$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\input{figures/fig_ranges.tex}
\caption{The three ranges of primes in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}.}
\label{fig:ranges}
\end{figure}

\emph{Large primes.} We have $\varphi_\mathcal{B}(z)\ge1-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{B},\,p\mid z}1/p$. The
number of $z\le n$ with $\sum_{p\in\mathcal{B},\,p\mid z}1/p>\eta$ is at most
\[
  \frac1\eta\sum_{z\le n}\ \sum_{p\in\mathcal{B},\,p\mid z}\frac1p\le\frac1\eta\sum_{p>Y}\frac n{p^2}
  \le\frac n{\eta Y}=\frac n{10^6}.
\]
For every other $z$ with $\rho'(z)<\theta$ we get $\rho'(S_z)\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)(1-\eta)<\theta\le
\theta'(1-\eta)$, so $\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)<\theta'/\rho'(S_z)$.

\emph{Middle primes.} Fix $S\subseteq P_{19}$ and let $Z_S=\{z\in[n]:z\bmod6\in R,\ S_z=S\}$. For
$D\subseteq\mathcal{T}$ the elements of $Z_S$ divisible by $\prod_{p\in D}p$ are the numbers
$(\prod_{p\in S\cup D}p)\,w$ with $w$ counted by $C_R(\lfloor n/\prod_{p\in S\cup D}p\rfloor;
P_{19}\setminus S)$; this is the argument of diagram~\eqref{cd:mult}, as the multiplier is
$\equiv\pm1\pmod6$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:count} this number is at most
\[
  \frac n6|R|\operatorname{dens}(S)\prod_{p\in D}\frac1p+|R|2^{6}.
\]
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:moment}, run inside $Z_S$, gives for every $m$ and $t>0$
\[
  \#\{z\in Z_S:\varphi_\mathcal{T}(z)\le t\}\le t^m\Big(\frac n6|R|\operatorname{dens}(S)
  \prod_{p\in\mathcal{T}}\Big(1+\frac{g_m(p)}p\Big)+|R|2^6\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T}}(1+g_m(p))\Big).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}, $\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T}}(1+g_m(p)/p)\le F_{23}(m)/(1-2m/199)\le\hat F_m$
for $m\in\mathcal{M}$. Take $t=\theta'/\rho'(S)$ and let $m_S\in\mathcal{M}$ minimize
$t^m\hat F_m$.

\emph{Summation.} Summing over $S$,
\[
  \mathrm{Bad}_R(n,\theta)\le\frac n{10^6}+\frac n6|R|\cert(\theta')+K,\qquad
  K=\sum_{S}\Big(\frac{\theta'}{\rho'(S)}\Big)^{m_S}6\cdot2^6\prod_{p\in\mathcal{T}}(1+g_{m_S}(p)),
\]
where $K$ does not depend on $n$. Put $N=\lceil6\cdot10^6K\rceil$. For $n\ge N$ we have $K\le
n/(6\cdot10^6)$, and $n/10^6+n/(6\cdot10^6)=(n/6)\cdot7\cdot10^{-6}\le(n/6)|R|\cdot7\cdot10^{-6}$.
Hence $\mathrm{Bad}_R(n,\theta)\le(n/6)|R|(\cert(\theta')+7\cdot10^{-6})\le(n/6)|R|\beta$.

\emph{The sixteen certificates.} For each row of Table~\ref{tab:cert}, $\cert(\theta')$ is a sum of
$64$ rationals, each a minimum of $14$ explicit rationals. The Lean kernel evaluates
$\cert(\theta')+7\cdot10^{-6}\le\beta$ exactly. Write $c(\theta',\beta)=\cert(\theta')+7\cdot
10^{-6}-\beta$ for the rational function and $c_\mathbb{R}$ for the same formula over $\mathbb{R}$.
Since $\iota\colon\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{R}$ is an embedding of ordered fields, the square
\begin{equation}\label{cd:cast}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
  \mathbb{Q}^{2} \arrow[r, "c"] \arrow[d, "\iota\times\iota"'] & \mathbb{Q} \arrow[d, "\iota"] \\
  \mathbb{R}^{2} \arrow[r, "c_\mathbb{R}"'] & \mathbb{R}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
commutes, because $c$ is built from field operations and minima, which $\iota$ preserves. As $\iota$
also preserves signs, the sixteen inequalities proved over $\mathbb{Q}$ hold over $\mathbb{R}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Triangles}\label{sec:triangle}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:triangle}
If $n\ge13$ and $A\subseteq[n]$ with $|A|>T(n)$, then $G(A)$ contains a triangle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $O$ be the set of odd elements of $A$ and $e$ the number of even integers in $[n]$ missing from
$A$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:odd}, $|O|\ge o(n)+1+e$. Use the blocks $\{6i+1,6i+3,6i+5\}$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:pair}. Odd numbers in one block are pairwise coprime, and each is coprime to $6i+2$
and to $6i+4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:blocks}). Suppose that $G(A)$ has no triangle. Then every block
contains at most two elements of $O$, and if it contains two, then neither $6i+2$ nor $6i+4$ lies in
$A$.

Let $F\subseteq O$ consist of the smallest element of $O$ in each block that meets $O$, and let
$P=O\setminus F$. Then $|F|\le\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor+1$, and $P$ has at most one element in each
block. For $v\in P$ in block $i$ the number $6i+2\le n$ is missing from $A$, and these numbers are
distinct, so $|P|\le e$. Hence
\[
  o(n)+1+e\le|O|=|F|+|P|\le\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor+1+e .
\]
Since $o(n)\ge\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$, equality holds throughout: $o(n)=\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$, so
$n\equiv1,2\pmod 6$, and $|P|=e$. For $v\in P$ in block $i$ we have $v\le n$ and $v\ge6i+3$, so
$6i+4\le n$ by the congruence of $n$, and $6i+4$ is missing from $A$ as well. The numbers $6i+2$ and
$6i+4$ obtained from all $v\in P$ are $2|P|$ distinct missing even numbers, so $2|P|\le e=|P|$ and
$e=0$. In particular $2\in A$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pair} there are distinct coprime $a,b\in O$, and
$a,b,2$ is a triangle.
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Sets that miss few even numbers prime to $3$}{Sets that miss few even numbers prime to 3}}\label{sec:few}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:few}
Let $n\ge N_0$ and $A\subseteq[n]$ with $|A|>T(n)$ and $70d\le n$. Then $G(A)$ contains a cycle of
length $2l+1$ for every integer $l$ with $1\le l\le o(n)$.
\end{theorem}

The constant $70$ is not critical.\footnote{The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:degree} needs
$\frac{14}{75}-\frac{3}{1000}-\frac1c>\frac16$ when $d\le n/c$, that is, $c>1000/17\approx58.8$; the
other steps need much less.}

Since $\lfloor n/6\rfloor\le o(n)$, this gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for these sets. Throughout
this section $n\ge N_0$, $A$ is as in Theorem~\ref{thm:few}, and all numbers lie in $[n]$. The
\emph{deficit} of $z$ is $a(z)=|N^*(n,z)\setminus A|\le d$, and $z$ is \emph{undamaged} if
$a(z)\le(n/8)\rho'(z)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:damaged}
At most $d$ integers in $[n]$ are damaged.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $d=0$ every integer is undamaged. Otherwise a damaged $z$ satisfies $(n/8)\rho'(z)<a(z)\le d$, so
$\rho'(z)<x=8d/n\le8/70<14/25$, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:hard}(a) there are at most
$xn/560=d/70\le d$ such $z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:connector}
If $y$ is odd and easy and $z$ is odd and undamaged, then $|N^*(n,\lcm(y,z))\cap A|\ge2+\rho'(z)n/280$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $m=\lcm(y,z)$ and $K=\omega'(m)$; then $m$ is odd and $m\le n^2$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:count},
$|N^*(n,m)|\ge(n/3)\rho'(m)-2^K$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:rho}, $\rho'(m)\ge\frac34\rho'(z)$. The
elements of $N^*(n,m)$ missing from $A$ lie in $N^*(n,z)\setminus A$, so there are at most
$a(z)\le(n/8)\rho'(z)$ of them. By Corollary~\ref{cor:large} and Lemma~\ref{lem:rho}(b),
$2^K\le n\rho'(m)/1000\le n\rho'(z)/1000$; in particular $n\rho'(z)\ge1000$. Hence
\[
  |N^*(n,m)\cap A|\ge n\rho'(z)\Big(\frac14-\frac18-\frac1{1000}\Big)\ge2+\frac{n\rho'(z)}{280},
\]
the last step because $\frac14-\frac18-\frac1{1000}-\frac1{280}>\frac1{10}$ and $n\rho'(z)\ge1000$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:degree}
Let $p,q$ be odd with $\rho'(\lcm(p,q))\ge14/25$, and let $W$ be a set with $|W|\le n/500+2$. Then
$N^*(n,\lcm(p,q))\setminus\big((\Es(n)\setminus A)\cup W\big)$ has at least $\lfloor n/6\rfloor+2$
elements.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Corollaries~\ref{cor:count} and \ref{cor:large}, the set has at least
$\frac n3\cdot\frac{14}{25}-\frac n{1000}-d-\frac n{500}-2\ge n\big(\frac{14}{75}-\frac1{1000}-
\frac1{70}-\frac1{500}\big)-2$ elements. The bracket exceeds $\frac16+0.0027$, and $0.0027n\ge4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:choose}
Let $O''$ be a finite set of odd numbers, $H$ its hard and $E$ its easy elements, with $|E|\ge2|H|+2$,
and let $l\ge4$ with $l-1\le|O''|$. Then there is $X_1\subseteq O''$ with $|X_1|=l-1$ whose sets $H_1$
of hard and $E_1$ of easy elements satisfy $|E_1|\ge2|H_1|+2$, $l\ge2|H_1|+4$ and $|H_1|\le|H|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $N=O''\setminus H$, so $E\subseteq N$. If $|N|\ge l-1$, take $X_1\subseteq N$ of size $l-1$
containing two elements of $E$; then $H_1=\emptyset$. Otherwise take $X_1=N\cup H'$ with $H'\subseteq
H$ of size $l-1-|N|\ge1$. Then $H_1=H'$ and $E_1=E$, so $|E_1|\ge2|H|+2\ge2|H_1|+2$, and
$l-1=|H_1|+|N|\ge|H_1|+|E|\ge3|H_1|+2$, whence $l\ge2|H_1|+4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:few}]
For $l=1$ apply Theorem~\ref{thm:triangle}; let $l\ge2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:odd} with
$D=\Es(n)\setminus A$, the set $O$ of odd elements of $A$ has at least $o(n)+1+d$ elements, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:damaged} the set $O'$ of its undamaged elements has at least $o(n)+1$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:pair} there are distinct coprime $a,b\in O'$. Let $O''=O'\setminus\{a,b\}$, so
$|O''|\ge o(n)-1$, and let $H$ and $E$ be its sets of hard and easy elements. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:hard}, $|H|\le n/1000$ and $|O''\setminus E|\le11n/70+11+n/1000$. With
$o(n)\ge n/6-1$ this gives
\[
  |E|\ge\frac n{105}-\frac n{1000}-13\ge\frac n{500}+2\ge2|H|+2 .
\]

\emph{$l=2$.} Take $y\in E$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:connector} the sets $N^*(n,\lcm(y,a))\cap A$ and
$N^*(n,\lcm(y,b))\cap A$ have at least two elements, so they contain distinct $w_a$ and $w_b$. Then
$a,b,w_b,y,w_a$ is a cycle of length $5$.

\emph{$l=3$.} Take distinct $y_a,y_b\in E$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:connector}, applied three times
($y_b$ is undamaged), each of the sets $N^*(n,\lcm(y_a,a))\cap A$, $N^*(n,\lcm(y_b,b))\cap A$ and
$N^*(n,\lcm(y_a,y_b))\cap A$ has at least three elements, so they contain distinct $w_0,w_1,w_2$. Then
$y_a,w_0,a,b,w_1,y_b,w_2$ is a cycle of length $7$.

\emph{$l\ge4$.} Choose $X_1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:choose}, with hard part $H_1$ and easy part $E_1$. Its
elements are odd, undamaged and different from $a,b$. Consider the set of \emph{slots}
\[
  \Sigma=\{(a,0),(b,1)\}\cup H_1\times\{0,1\},\qquad|\Sigma|=2|H_1|+2 ,
\]
and call the first coordinate of a slot its owner. Since $|E_1|\ge|\Sigma|$, there is an injective
choice of \emph{partners} $\pi(\sigma)\in E_1$. For a slot $\sigma$ with owner $z$ let
$C_\sigma=N^*(n,\lcm(\pi(\sigma),z))\cap A$, and order the slots by $\rho'$ of their owners. The slots
whose owner $z'$ has $\rho'(z')\le\rho'(z)$ number at most $2+2\#\{c\in H_1:\rho'(c)\le\rho'(z)\}\le
2+\rho'(z)n/280$, by Corollary~\ref{cor:hard}(a) when $\rho'(z)\le14/25$ and by $|H_1|\le n/1000$
otherwise. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:connector} and \ref{lem:greedy} there are distinct \emph{connectors}
$\omega(\sigma)\in C_\sigma$. Form the sequences
\begin{align*}
  P_a&=\big(\pi(a,0),\,\omega(a,0),\,a,\,b,\,\omega(b,1),\,\pi(b,1)\big),\\
  P_c&=\big(\pi(c,0),\,\omega(c,0),\,c,\,\omega(c,1),\,\pi(c,1)\big)\quad(c\in H_1),\\
  P_c&=(c)\quad(c\in R),\qquad R=X_1\setminus\big(H_1\cup\pi(\Sigma)\big),
\end{align*}
indexed by $X=\{a\}\cup H_1\cup R$ (Figure~\ref{fig:long}). Consecutive terms are coprime by the
choice of the connectors and since $a,b$ are coprime. The sequences are pairwise disjoint: the odd
terms are the distinct elements $a$, $b$, $\pi(\sigma)$ and the elements of $H_1\cup R$, and the even
terms are the distinct connectors. We have $|R|=l-1-|H_1|-|\Sigma|=l-3-3|H_1|$, hence
$|X|=1+|H_1|+|R|=l-2-2|H_1|\ge2$ and $|X|\le l\le o(n)\le k$, where $k=\lfloor n/6\rfloor+2$.

The pool is $B=(\Es(n)\cap A)\setminus\omega(\Sigma)$; it is disjoint from all $P_c$ and
$|B|\le|\Es(n)|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor-\lfloor n/6\rfloor\le2k-2$. The ends of $P_a$ and $P_c$ $(c\in
H_1)$ are two easy numbers, with $\rho'$ of their least common multiple at least $(3/4)^2>14/25$;
the single vertices $c\in R$ are not hard. Lemma~\ref{lem:degree} with $W=\omega(\Sigma)$, where
$|W|=2|H_1|+2\le n/500+2$, shows that each sequence has at least $k$ attached elements in $B$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:assembly} gives a cycle of length
\[
  6+5|H_1|+|R|+|X|=7+6|H_1|+2|R|=2l+1. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Two constructions for sets that miss many even numbers prime to $3$}{Two constructions for sets that miss many even numbers prime to 3}}\label{sec:constructions}

In this section and the next $n\ge N_1$. For $A\subseteq[n]$ and $t\in\mathbb{R}$ let
\[
  \OA(t)=\{z\in A:z\text{ odd},\ \rho'(z)\ge t\},\qquad\UA(t)=\{z\in A\cap U(n):\rho'(z)\ge t\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:countbig}
Let $1\le m\le n^2$. For odd $m$, $|N^*(n,m)|\ge(n/3)\rho'(m)-n/10^6$. If $R$ is symmetric and
nonempty, and $m$ and $R$ satisfy the hypothesis of Corollary~\ref{cor:count}, then
$\#\{w\in[n]:w\bmod6\in R,\ \gcd(w,m)=1\}\ge(n/6)|R|\rho'(m)-|R|n/10^6$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Corollaries~\ref{cor:count} and \ref{cor:large}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[unit construction]\label{prop:unit}
Let $A\subseteq[n]$, let $2\le l\le k$ be integers and $\theta_T,r_1,t_2$ reals with $r_1\ge0$ and
$\theta_T\le r_1t_2$. Suppose that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(T\arabic*), nosep]
\item $|\UA(\theta_T)|\ge l+1$;
\item $\UA(r_1)\neq\emptyset$;
\item $2+2n/10^6+|U(n)\setminus A|+\#\{z\in U(n):\rho'(z)<t_2\}\le(n/3)r_1$;
\item $k+\mu_T+4n/10^6\le(2n/3)\theta_T$;
\item $T(n)+2\le2k+\mu_T$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $2l+1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $w\in\UA(r_1)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:countbig} with $R=\{1,5\}$ and $m=w$, at least
$(n/3)\rho'(w)-2n/10^6\ge(n/3)r_1-2n/10^6$ units of $[n]$ are coprime to $w$. Removing those missing
from $A$ and those with $\rho'<t_2$ leaves at least two by (T3), so there is $w'\ne w$ in $\UA(t_2)$
coprime to $w$. By (T1) there is a set $S\subseteq\UA(\theta_T)\setminus\{w,w'\}$ of $l-1$ units.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:assembly} to the sequences $(w,w')$ and $(c)$, $c\in S$, indexed by
$X=\{w\}\cup S$, with pool $B=M(n)\cap A$ (Figure~\ref{fig:unit}). Here $|X|=l\le k$ and
$|B|=T(n)-\mu_T\le2k-2$ by (T5). The sequences consist of units, so they avoid $B$. For the ends
$p,q$ of a sequence we have $\rho'(\lcm(p,q))\ge\theta_T$: for $(w,w')$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rho}(c),
$\rho'(\lcm(w,w'))\ge r_1t_2\ge\theta_T$, and for $(c)$ by the choice of $S$. As $\lcm(p,q)$ is prime
to $6$, Lemma~\ref{lem:countbig} with $R=\{0,2,3,4\}$ gives at least $(2n/3)\theta_T-4n/10^6$
elements of $M(n)$ coprime to $p$ and $q$; at most $\mu_T$ of them are missing from $A$, so by (T4)
each sequence has at least $k$ attached elements. The cycle has length $(2+(l-1))+l=2l+1$.
\end{proof}

pool vertices from $M(n)\cap A$.}
\label{fig:unit}
\end{figure}

\begin{proposition}[giant-path construction]\label{prop:gp}
Let $A\subseteq[n]$, let $l,k\ge2$ be integers and $s,t_1,\theta,\theta_w,s_w$ reals with
$t_1,\theta_w\ge0$, $s_w\le s$, and $s_w\le\theta_w$ if $k<l$. Suppose that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*), nosep]
\item $|\OA(s)|\ge o(n)+1$;
\item $|\OA(t_1)|\ge3$;
\item $\theta\le t_1s$;
\item if $k<l$, then $|\UA(\theta_w)|\ge l-k+2$;
\item $d+1+n/10^6\le(n/3)t_1s_w$;
\item if $k<l$, then $(l-k-1)+\mu_6+n/10^6\le(n/6)\theta_ws_w$;
\item $|\OA(\theta)|\ge l+1$;
\item $|\Es(n)|+1\le2k+d$;
\item $k+d+1+n/10^6\le(n/3)\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $2l+1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By (H1) and Lemma~\ref{lem:pair} there are distinct coprime odd $x,x'\in\OA(s)$. One of them is not a
multiple of $3$ (two multiples of $3$ are not coprime) and is therefore a unit; call it $w^\circ$ and
the other $g_2$. By (H2) there is $g_1\in\OA(t_1)\setminus\{w^\circ,g_2\}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:rho}(c), $\rho'(\lcm(g_1,g_2))\ge t_1s\ge\theta$ by (H3). For every odd $p$ with
$\rho'(p)\ge s_w$ we have $\rho'(\lcm(g_1,p))\ge t_1s_w$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:countbig} with (H5)
gives
\[
  |N^*(n,\lcm(g_1,p))\cap A|\ge(n/3)t_1s_w-n/10^6-d\ge1. \tag{$*$}
\]
Likewise, for odd $p,q$ with $\rho'(\lcm(p,q))\ge\theta$, the set $(\Es(n)\cap A)\setminus\{e\}$, for
any $e$, contains at least $(n/3)\theta-n/10^6-d-1\ge k$ elements coprime to $p$ and $q$, by (H9).

\emph{$l=2$.} By $(*)$ there is $e_1\in N^*(n,\lcm(g_1,w^\circ))\cap A$, and there is
$\beta\in\Es(n)\cap A$, $\beta\ne e_1$, coprime to $g_1$ and $g_2$. Then $g_1,e_1,w^\circ,g_2,\beta$
is a cycle of length $5$.

\emph{$l\ge3$.} Let $Q=1$ if $l\le k$ and $Q=l-k$ otherwise; then $1\le Q\le l-2$ and $l-Q\le k$. Put
$w_{Q-1}=w^\circ$. If $Q\ge2$, then $k<l$ and $Q-1=l-k-1$, and (H4) provides distinct units
$w_0,\dots,w_{Q-2}\in\UA(\theta_w)\setminus\{g_1,g_2,w^\circ\}$. All $w_i$ satisfy $\rho'(w_i)\ge
s_w$. By $(*)$ there is $e_1\in N^*(n,\lcm(g_1,w_0))\cap A$. If $Q\ge2$, then for $i\le Q-2$ the number
$\lcm(w_i,w_{i+1})$ is prime to $6$ and has $\rho'\ge\theta_ws_w$; Lemma~\ref{lem:countbig} with
$R=\{0\}$ gives at least $(n/6)\theta_ws_w-n/10^6-\mu_6\ge Q-1$ elements of $M_6(n)\cap A$ coprime to
$w_iw_{i+1}$, by (H6), and Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy} yields distinct such elements $y_i$. The
\emph{giant path}
\[
  \Gamma=(g_1,\,e_1,\,w_0,\,y_0,\,w_1,\,y_1,\,\dots,\,y_{Q-2},\,w_{Q-1},\,g_2)
\]
has $2Q+2$ distinct terms, consecutive terms being coprime (Figure~\ref{fig:giant}). Its odd terms
form a set $W_{\mathrm{odd}}$ of at most $Q+2$ elements, so by (H7) there is $S\subseteq\OA(\theta)
\setminus W_{\mathrm{odd}}$ with $|S|=l-1-Q$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:assembly} to $\Gamma$ and the sequences $(c)$, $c\in S$, indexed by
$X=\{g_1\}\cup S$, with pool $B=(\Es(n)\cap A)\setminus\{e_1\}$ and the integer $k$. Here
$2\le|X|=l-Q\le k$, and $|B|=|\Es(n)|-d-1\le2k-2$ by (H8). The pool contains no term of the
sequences: odd numbers, multiples of $6$ and $e_1$ are excluded. The ends of every sequence have a
least common multiple with $\rho'\ge\theta$, so each has at least $k$ attached elements. The cycle has
length $(2Q+2)+(l-1-Q)+(l-Q)=2l+1$.
\end{proof}

pool $\Es(n)\cap A$ cannot carry when $k<l$.}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}{Proof of Theorem 1.1}}\label{sec:boxes}

Let $\eps=5\cdot10^{-4}$. We say that a pair $(g,\beta)$ \emph{holds at $n$} if
$\mathrm{Bad}_R(n,g)\le(n/6)|R|\beta$ for every nonempty symmetric $R$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}
there is $N_c$ such that all sixteen pairs of Table~\ref{tab:cert} hold at every $n\ge N_c$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:basic}
If $A\subseteq[n]$ and $|A|>T(n)$, then $|\{z\in A:z\text{ odd}\}|\ge o(n)+1+d+\mu_6$, $u\ge\mu_T+1$,
$d+\mu_6\le\mu_T$, $|U(n)\setminus A|+u+T(n)=n$ and $\mu_T\le n/3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first bound is Lemma~\ref{lem:odd} with $D=(\Es(n)\cup M_6(n))\setminus A$. The elements of $A$
outside $U(n)$ lie in $M(n)\cap A$, which has $T(n)-\mu_T$ elements, so $u\ge T(n)+1-(T(n)-\mu_T)$.
The third relation holds because $\Es(n)$ and $M_6(n)$ are disjoint subsets of $M(n)$, and the fourth
because $|U(n)|=n-T(n)$. Finally $\mu_T+1\le u\le|U(n)|$, and $|U(n)|-1\le n/3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:certcount}
If $(g,\beta)$ holds at $n$ and $t\le g$, then for every $A\subseteq[n]$,
$|\OA(t)|\ge|\{z\in A:z\text{ odd}\}|-(n/2)\beta$, $|\UA(t)|\ge u-(n/3)\beta$ and
$\#\{z\in U(n):\rho'(z)<t\}\le(n/3)\beta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the definition with $R=\{1,3,5\}$ and $R=\{1,5\}$; an element with $\rho'<t$ has $\rho'<g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[unit boxes]\label{lem:tbox}
Let $|A|>T(n)$ and $2\le l\le n/6$. Suppose that $\lambda_0n\le\mu_T\le\lambda_1n+1$, that
$(g_T,\beta_T)$, $(g_r,\beta_r)$, $(g_t,\beta_t)$ hold at $n$ with $\theta_T\le g_T$, $r_1\le g_r$,
$t_2\le g_t$, $r_1\ge0$, and that
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\tfrac12+\tfrac34\lambda_1+\eps\le\theta_T,\qquad
  \tfrac16+\tfrac13\beta_T+\eps\le\lambda_0,\qquad
  \tfrac13\beta_r+\eps\le\lambda_0,\\
  &\tfrac13\beta_t+\tfrac13(1-r_1)+\eps\le\lambda_0,\qquad
  \theta_T\le r_1t_2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Then $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $2l+1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $k=\lfloor(T(n)-\mu_T+3)/2\rfloor$ and check the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:unit}. Write
$n=6q+r$ with $0\le r<6$; then $q\ge l$ and a direct check gives $2T(n)-n\ge2q-1$ and $2n-3\le3T(n)\le2n+3$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:basic}, $\mu_T+1\le u\le n-T(n)$, so $T(n)-\mu_T\ge2T(n)-n+1\ge2q$ and $k\ge q+1>l$.
(T5) holds as $2k\ge T(n)-\mu_T+2$. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:basic} and \ref{lem:certcount},
$|\UA(\theta_T)|\ge\mu_T+1-(n/3)\beta_T\ge(1/6+\eps)n+1\ge l+1$ and $|\UA(r_1)|\ge(\lambda_0-
\beta_r/3)n+1\ge1$, which are (T1) and (T2). For (T3), $|U(n)\setminus A|=n-T(n)-u\le n/3+1-\mu_T-1
\le n/3-\lambda_0n$ and the bad units number at most $(n/3)\beta_t$, so the left side of (T3) is at
most $2+2n/10^6+n/3-\lambda_0n+(n/3)\beta_t\le(n/3)r_1+2+2n/10^6-\eps n\le(n/3)r_1$. For (T4),
$k+\mu_T\le(T(n)+3+\mu_T)/2\le n/3+\lambda_1n/2+5/2$, while $(2n/3)\theta_T\ge n/3+\lambda_1n/2+
(2/3)\eps n$, and $(2/3)\eps n\ge5/2+4n/10^6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[giant-path boxes]\label{lem:gpbox}
Let $|A|>T(n)$ and $2\le l\le n/6$. Suppose that $\delta_0n\le3d\le\delta_1n$ and $a_0n\le\mu_6\le
a_1n$ with $\delta_1\le9/10$; that $(g_s,\beta_s)$, $(g_1,\beta_1)$, $(g_\theta,\beta_\theta)$,
$(g_w,\beta_w)$ hold at $n$ with $s\le g_s$, $t_1\le g_1$, $\theta\le g_\theta$, $\theta_w\le g_w$; that
$t_1,\theta_w,\kappa\ge0$ and $s_w\le s$; and that
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\tfrac12+\tfrac12\delta_1+3\kappa+\eps\le\theta,\quad
  \tfrac12\beta_s+\eps\le\tfrac13\delta_0+a_0,\quad
  \tfrac12\beta_1+\eps\le\tfrac16+\tfrac13\delta_0+a_0,\\
  &\theta\le t_1s,\quad
  \delta_1+\eps\le t_1s_w,\quad
  \tfrac12\beta_\theta+\eps\le\tfrac13\delta_0+a_0 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Suppose further that, unless $\kappa\ge\delta_1/6+\eps$,
\[
  \tfrac13\beta_w+\eps\le\tfrac16\delta_0+a_0+\kappa,\qquad
  \tfrac16\delta_1-\kappa+a_1+\eps\le\tfrac16\theta_ws_w,\qquad s_w\le\theta_w .
\]
Then $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $2l+1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $e_n=|\Es(n)|$, so $(n-3)/3\le e_n\le(n+3)/3$, and let $k=\lfloor(e_n-d+2)/2\rfloor+\lfloor\kappa
n\rfloor$. We check the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:gp}. (H8) holds by the choice of $k$, and
$k\ge(e_n-d+1)/2\ge2$ since $d\le3n/10$. From $k\ge(e_n-d+1)/2+\kappa n-1\ge n/6-d/2+\kappa n-1$ we get:
if $\kappa\ge\delta_1/6+\eps$, then $k\ge n/6+\eps n-1>l$; so $k<l$ implies $\kappa<\delta_1/6+
\eps$, and the second group of inequalities is available whenever $k<l$. In every case $l-k+2\le d/2-\kappa
n+3$.

(H9): $k+d\le(e_n+2+2\kappa n+d)/2\le n/6+\delta_1n/6+\kappa n+3/2$, and $(n/3)\theta\ge n/6+\delta_1n/6
+\kappa n+\eps n/3$. (H1), (H2), (H7): by Lemmas~\ref{lem:basic} and \ref{lem:certcount},
$|\OA(t)|\ge o(n)+1+(\delta_0/3+a_0-\beta/2)n$ for each of the pairs used; this is at least $o(n)+1$ for
$t=s$ and $t=\theta$, which gives (H1) and (H7) as $l\le\lfloor n/6\rfloor\le o(n)$, and at least
$o(n)+1+(\eps-1/6)n\ge3$ for $t=t_1$. (H3) is assumed. (H5): $d+1+n/10^6\le\delta_1n/3+1+n/10^6\le
(n/3)t_1s_w$. (H4) for $k<l$: $|\UA(\theta_w)|\ge u-(n/3)\beta_w\ge d+\mu_6+1-(n/3)\beta_w$, and
$d+\mu_6+1-(n/3)\beta_w-(d/2-\kappa n+3)\ge(\delta_0/6+a_0+\kappa-\beta_w/3)n-2\ge0$. (H6) for $k<l$:
$(l-k-1)+\mu_6\le d/2-\kappa n+a_1n\le(\delta_1/6-\kappa+a_1)n\le(n/6)\theta_ws_w-\eps n$.
\end{proof}

Table~\ref{tab:gpboxes}; Figure~\ref{fig:boxes} shows the cover.\footnote{The boxes were found by a computer search over grids of parameters. The
search is not part of the proof: each row only has to satisfy the inequalities of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tbox} or Lemma~\ref{lem:gpbox}, and these are checked exactly.} In each row the thresholds $g$ are
the smallest certificate thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:cert} that are at least the corresponding
parameter, and $\beta$ is the certified value at $g$. In mode~II we put $s_w=\min(\theta_w,s)$; in
mode~I we have $\kappa=\delta_1/6+\eps$, the last three inequalities are not needed, and we put

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $n_0=\max(N_1,N_c)$; note $N_1\ge N_0\ge13$. Let $n\ge n_0$, $A\subseteq[n]$ with $|A|>T(n)$, and
$L=2l+1$ odd with $3\le L\le n/3+1$, so that $1\le l\le\lfloor n/6\rfloor\le o(n)$. If $l=1$, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:triangle}; if $70d\le n$, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:few}. Assume now $l\ge2$ and
$70d>n$, so that $\delta=3d/n>3/70$.

If $\mu_T\ge9n/50$, then $9/50\le\mu_T/n\le1/3$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:basic}. Split according to
$\mu_T\le\frac{23}{100}n+1$, $\mu_T\le\frac{33}{100}n+1$ or neither; in each case one row of
Table~\ref{tab:tboxes} satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:tbox}, whose inequalities are
rational and are checked directly.

If $\mu_T<9n/50$, then $d\le\mu_T$ gives $\delta<27/50$, and $\mu_6\le\mu_T-d$ gives $\mu_6/n<9/50-
\delta/3\le9/50-\delta_0/3$ for every $\delta_0\le\delta$. The values of $\delta$ in $[3/70,27/50]$ are
split into the ten intervals $[\delta_0,\delta_1]$ of Table~\ref{tab:gpboxes}, and for each of them the
values of $\mu_6/n$ in $[0,9/50-\delta_0/3]$ into the intervals $[a_0,a_1]$ listed. Every row satisfies
the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:gpbox}. In all cases $G(A)$ contains a cycle of length $L$.
\end{proof}

\section{Formal verification}\label{sec:lean}

The Lean development consists of $28$ files and about $6600$ lines; it builds with Lean~4.34.1 and
Mathlib~4.34.1. The coprime graph is defined in Mathlib's graph library: the vertices are the
elements of a finite set $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$, two vertices are adjacent when they are distinct and
coprime, and a cycle of length $L$ is a closed walk of length $L$ that repeats no edge and no vertex
other than its starting point.\footnote{This is Mathlib's notion \texttt{SimpleGraph.Walk.IsCycle}.
Such a walk has length at least $3$, and its vertices, read in order, form a sequence
$v_1,\dots,v_L$ as in the definition given in the introduction.}
The statement proved is
\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=lean]
def Question1 : Prop :=
  ∃ n₀ : ℕ, ∀ n ≥ n₀, ∀ A : Finset ℕ, A ⊆ Finset.Icc 1 n → T n < A.card →
    ∀ L : ℕ, Odd L → 3 ≤ L → L ≤ n / 3 + 1 → HasCycleOfLength (coprimeGraph A) L

theorem question1 : Question1
\end{lstlisting}
\end{minipage}
\par\noindent where \texttt{T n = n / 2 + n / 3 - n / 6} and \texttt{/} is division with rounding down; for an
integer $L$ the condition $L\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor+1$ is the same as $L\le n/3+1$. The command
\texttt{\#print axioms question1} reports \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}. No
\texttt{sorry}, no additional axiom and no compiled evaluation (\texttt{native\_decide}) occurs. The
inequality \eqref{eq:F5}, its analogue for $F_{11}(54)$, the bounds for $\hat F_m$ and the sixteen
certificates are evaluated in exact rational arithmetic by \texttt{decide +kernel}, and the box
inequalities by the tactic \texttt{norm\_num}. The file \texttt{Closure.lean}, which contains the case
analysis of Section~\ref{sec:boxes}, is generated from the box data by a script and is checked by Lean
like every other file.

The repository also contains the scripts that reproduce every number and figure of this paper: the
box search and the generator of \texttt{Closure.lean} (Python), the tables, which are recomputed in
exact rational arithmetic from the Lean sources (Python), and the empirical densities of
Figure~\ref{fig:dens} (C).
